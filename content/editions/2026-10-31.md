# Islamic Finance Daily Brief — Saturday, October 31, 2026

Assalamu alaikum! Today's Islamic finance stories — and what to learn from them.

## Today's stories

### 🌍 1. The UAE's October Treasury sukuk auction draws bids worth AED4.54 billion

The UAE Ministry of Finance, working with the Central Bank of the UAE, has completed its October 2026 auction of dirham-denominated Islamic Treasury Sukuk and Treasury Bonds. Total issuance came to AED1.1 billion, while bids from primary dealers reached AED4.54 billion. The new T-Sukuk tranche matures in October 2029 with a yield to maturity of 4.95%, and the T-Bond tranche matures in January 2031 at 5.05%, and both are listed on Nasdaq Dubai.

**What to learn:** A T-Sukuk is a certificate issued by a government under Islamic principles. The government raises money for a fixed period, and the holder receives a return from an underlying Shariah-compliant arrangement rather than interest. The T-Bond tranche in the same auction is a conventional bond, and it pays interest. Governments run regular auctions like this one for two reasons. The first is funding. The second is building a yield curve, which is a published set of yields for borrowing over different periods, from short to long. Once that set exists, banks, companies, and other borrowers can price their own borrowing against it. The auction works through primary dealers, which are banks appointed to bid in government auctions. In October, dealers bid AED4.54 billion for AED1.1 billion on offer. Dividing 4.54 by 1.1 gives about 4.1, so demand was about 4.1 times supply. That oversubscription is one reason the pricing came in tight. The T-Sukuk yield sits 19 basis points above comparable US Treasuries, and the T-Bond yield 26 basis points above. One basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so 19 basis points is 0.19 percentage points. The people who run the auction are the Ministry of Finance as issuer and the Central Bank of the UAE as issuing and payment agent, which means the central bank runs the auction mechanics and moves the money. The T-Sukuk and T-Bonds are listed on Nasdaq Dubai, so investors can trade them after the auction instead of holding them to maturity. For an ordinary investor in the UAE who wants a government-backed Shariah-compliant instrument in dirhams, the auction adds to the supply of exactly that. For a reader in the USA, the comparison is direct. US Treasury bills and bonds pay interest, so they are not Shariah-compliant instruments. A US investor who wants government-backed Shariah-compliant exposure looks instead to sukuk funds and ETFs that hold sukuk, since the US Treasury issues no sukuk.
