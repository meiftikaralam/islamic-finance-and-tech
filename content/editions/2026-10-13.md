### Mudaraba — the partnership where one side brings the money and the other brings the skill

**What it is:** Mudaraba is a partnership with two roles. One partner, the rabb al-mal, provides the capital. The other, the mudarib, provides the work — the skill, time, and management. Profits are split by a percentage the two agree on before starting. Losses are borne by the capital provider, since their money is what was lost; the manager loses their effort, unless they were careless or broke the agreement, in which case they can be held liable.

**How it works:** Say you have savings but no investing skill, and a friend is a skilled trader. You provide $50,000 and agree to split profits 50/50. Your friend trades halal stocks with your money. If the portfolio earns $10,000, you each take $5,000. If it loses $10,000, you absorb the loss and your friend walks away with nothing for their time — provided they followed the agreed rules. The capital provider stays out of day-to-day decisions; the agreement defines what the manager may and may not do. (Mudaraba was mentioned in passing in the Oct 10 edition's takaful explainer; this is the full picture.)

**A real-world example:** Islamic banks use mudaraba for savings accounts: depositors are the capital providers and the bank is the manager, investing the pooled deposits and sharing profits with depositors by a declared ratio. It is also the classical structure behind venture-style financing, where an investor funds an entrepreneur's business for a share of the profits.
