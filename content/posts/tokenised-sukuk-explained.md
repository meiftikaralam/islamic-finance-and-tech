## What a tokenised sukuk is

Start with a regular sukuk. A sukuk is a certificate tied to a real asset — a building, a road, a pool of leases. Investors put money in, the asset earns profit, and investors get a share of that profit. Returns come from the asset, never from interest.

A tokenised sukuk is the same certificate, recorded as digital tokens on a blockchain instead of as entries in a bank's clearing system. Each token represents a slice of the sukuk. Buying, holding, and selling happen on the blockchain ledger, which every participant can inspect.

The payment terms are written as smart contracts — programs on the blockchain that carry out the sukuk's rules automatically. When a profit payment is due, the program sends it to each token holder. No clerk processes it by hand.

## How it differs from a regular sukuk

The economics are identical: same asset, same profit share, same Shariah structure. What changes is the plumbing.

Three practical differences:

1. **Settlement is faster.** A regular sukuk trade can take days to settle through banks and clearing houses. On a blockchain, buyer and seller swap tokens and money in minutes.
2. **Paperwork shrinks.** Ownership, payment schedules, and transfers live in one shared ledger instead of in separate records at each bank involved.
3. **Smaller slices become possible.** Because tokens can be split finely, a sukuk could one day be sold in small pieces to ordinary investors. Today that is still mostly theory — the tokenised sukuk issued so far have gone to banks and institutions, not to retail buyers.

## One real-life example: the ADI Foundation–Tokinvest deal

On October 6, 2026, the ADI Foundation and Tokinvest signed an agreement to work toward a $100 million tokenised sukuk. [GCC Business News](https://www.gccbusinessnews.com/adi-foundation-and-tokinvest-collaboration/) reported the deal.

The two sides split the work:

- **ADI Foundation** runs ADI Chain, a blockchain built for banks and institutions to issue and settle digital assets. It brings the asset pipeline and the technology.
- **Tokinvest** is a Dubai investment platform regulated by VARA, the UAE's virtual-assets regulator. It handles the regulated part: structuring the product, checking investors, and distributing it. It has done this before with a tokenised money-market fund from Franklin Templeton, a digital bond issued in Luxembourg, and tokenised UK residential property. [Arab Times](https://arabtimesnews.com/adi-foundation-tokinvest-collaborate-on-100-million-tokenised-sukuk/)

The plan: take existing sukuk issued by UAE entities, convert them into tokens on ADI Chain, and sell them through regulated distribution partners. [Finance News International](https://www.financenewsint.com/articles/adi-foundation-tokinvest-sign-mo-u-targeting-100-million-tokenized-sukuk) notes the distribution stays inside regulated channels rather than open crypto markets.

Be clear about what this is and is not. The October 6 document is a memorandum of understanding — an agreement to explore and build a framework. No $100 million tokenised sukuk has been issued under it yet. The "primary target" language in the announcement means exactly that: a target.

## Options in the USA

None for ordinary investors today. No tokenised sukuk product is currently offered to US retail buyers. The activity so far is institutional: earlier in 2026, Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund Khazanah issued a RM100 million tokenised sukuk with CIMB and Maybank participating, sold to institutions. [ainvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/sukuk-token-cimb-put-money-ledger-2608/)

A US learner who wants sukuk exposure now buys regular sukuk funds instead — the [sukuk guide](/lab/sukuk-guide.html) lists verified options.

## Options in the UK, EU, and Australia

The same answer: no tokenised sukuk is available to ordinary investors in these regions today. The live work is happening in the UAE and Malaysia, aimed at institutions through regulated channels.

What you can do as a learner: follow the ADI–Tokinvest build-out and Malaysia's pilots. When tokenised products eventually reach retail, the questions to ask will be the familiar ones — who certified the Shariah compliance, what asset backs the tokens, and what the fees are. The technology is new; the due diligence is not.
