# SS 2: Debit Card, Charge Card and Credit Card

## What this standard is about

Note: AAOIFI later issued SS 61 (Payment Cards), which replaces this standard with updated rules. The summary below covers SS 2 as originally published.

The standard covers the three card types a bank can issue. A debit card spends the customer's own money; the account is debited immediately and no credit is given. A charge card lets the customer pay now and settle the full bill later, usually each month. A credit card gives a revolving credit line the customer can repay in installments.

## Why it exists

Cards are central to modern payments. The standard separates permissible cards from interest-based credit. It also sets rules on the fees a bank may charge, the commission merchants pay, and how cards may be used.

## The key rules, simply put

- Debit cards are permissible, as long as the customer stays within their available balance and no interest arises from the transaction.
- Charge cards are permissible, as long as the customer is not obliged to pay interest if they are late paying the bill.
- Credit cards with an interest-bearing revolving credit facility are not permissible. If a credit card is issued, it must be on an interest-free basis (qard hasan).
- The bank may charge membership, renewal, or replacement fees. These pay for the right to hold the card and use its services.
- The bank may charge the merchant a commission on card purchases. This is treated as a brokerage, marketing, and debt-collection service charge.
- Cards must not be used for purposes prohibited by Shariah. The bank may withdraw the card if the customer violates this.
- Buying gold, silver, or currency with a debit card counts as taking possession, because the accounting entry is treated as constructive possession.
- A customer may withdraw more than their available balance if the bank agrees and no interest is stipulated. This is treated as a permissible loan.

## An everyday example

A customer pays for groceries with a debit card. The money leaves their account straight away. No credit is extended and no interest is charged. The card works simply as a payment tool.

## Words to know

- **Qard hasan** — a benevolent, interest-free loan.
- **Riba** — interest; any guaranteed extra on a loan. Not allowed in Islamic finance.
- **Revolving credit** — a credit line the customer can reuse as it is repaid.
- **Constructive possession** — treated as received, for example through an accounting entry.
