# SS 4: Settlement of Debts by Set-Off

## What this standard is about

The standard covers set-off (muqassah). When two parties are both creditor and debtor to each other, their debts can be netted against each other instead of being paid separately. It covers set-off done by mutual agreement, and set-off that takes effect automatically once its conditions are met.

## Why it exists

Set-off is a practical way to settle mutual debts without moving money twice. The standard sets conditions so that the netting does not become a back door to riba (interest) or other Shariah violations.

## The key rules, simply put

- Each party must be a creditor and a debtor at the same time.
- Both parties must agree to the set-off.
- The set-off must not be arranged in a way that breaks a Shariah rule, such as riba.
- The two debts do not need to be the same kind, type, description, or maturity date.
- If the amounts are not equal, the smaller debt cancels an equal part of the larger one. The party owed the larger amount can claim the remaining balance.

## An everyday example

A owes B $5,000 and B owes A $3,000. They agree to set off the debts. The $3,000 cancels out, and A now owes B only $2,000.

## Words to know

- **Set-off (muqassah)** — canceling mutual debts against each other.
- **Riba** — interest; any guaranteed extra on a loan or debt. Not allowed in Islamic finance.
