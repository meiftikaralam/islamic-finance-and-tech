Daily Edition Rules

The complete standing rules for the Islamic Finance Daily Brief. Every edition

is built against this document. If a rule changes, this page changes first.

Purpose and audience

A daily email newsletter for people learning Islamic finance. Readers are in

the USA, UK, Australia, and Europe. The goal: every story leaves the reader

understanding something new.

Schedule and approval

  • A draft is produced every morning around 7:36 AM (reader's timezone).
  • Iftikar reviews every edition. Nothing is final or published without his

explicit approval.

  • Nothing is emailed automatically. Iftikar sends the approved edition

manually through Kit.

Regions

  • Every story must be relevant to the USA, UK, Australia, or Europe.
  • Stories solely about other regions are skipped, unless they directly

affect one of these audiences.

Edition structure

  1. 1. Five news stories. At least three are USA-focused. The rest come from

the UK, Australia, or Europe. Each story is flagged with its region

(🇺🇸 / 🇬🇧 / 🇦🇺 / 🇪🇺).

  1. 2. AI Watch (conditional). At most one story, and only on days with a

genuine AI × Islamic finance story — for example AI-driven Shariah

screening, halal robo-advisors, or AI in sukuk governance. If there is no

genuine story, the section is omitted entirely. Generic AI news never

qualifies.

  1. 3. Learn Islamic Finance. One beginner-friendly explainer per edition,

rotated from a queue so concepts do not repeat.

Story ranking

Each candidate story is scored 1–5 on five factors, then weighted:

  • US relevance ×6 (max 30)
  • Teaches something new ×4 (max 20)
  • Technology impact ×4 (max 20)
  • Development attention (would a reader tracking Islamic finance stop to

read this?) ×4 (max 20)

  • Freshness ×2 (max 10)

Total: 100 points. Only stories scoring 60 or above are selected. Slots

that fall below 60 go to guidance pieces (see below). Guidance pieces are not

scored. Every candidate's scores are shown in the morning report so the

ranking can be audited.

Story format

Each story has three parts, in this order:

  1. 1. Summary — 2–3 sentences in original words. Never copy article text.
  2. 2. What to learn — 4–6 short plain sentences teaching the concept behind

the news: the underlying concept in basic terms, how it works, why this

route was used, what alternatives exist, and the practical impact. This is

the core value of the newsletter.

  1. 3. Source link — the direct URL of the story page. Every story must have

one.

Plain-language bar

  • Super-simple English. Short sentences. No jargon without an explanation.
  • Never use "X, not Y" contrast flourishes.
  • Never use "think of it as X" analogies.
  • No staged openers, no em-dash habit, no bold spam, no marketing language.
  • No bluff. If a fact is not verified with a source, it does not go in.

Educational only

  • For educational purposes only — not investment advice.
  • Never recommend buying, selling, or holding any security.
  • Companies and funds are examples for learning, never endorsements.
  • AI-assisted content can contain mistakes. Readers are told to do their own

research and consult a qualified professional.

  • Every edition ends with the full disclaimer, word for word:
For educational purposes only — not investment advice. Nothing here is a
recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security. Iftikar is not a licensed
financial advisor. AI-assisted content can contain mistakes; do your own
research and consult a qualified professional before making financial
decisions.

Thin-news fallback

If fewer than five solid region-relevant stories are found, the empty slots

are filled with evergreen 💡 GUIDANCE pieces — short explainers on finance,

business finance, or Islamic finance topics. Each carries the 💡 GUIDANCE label.

Freshness and no repeats

  • Prefer stories from the last 24 hours; fall back to 48 hours on quiet days.
  • Do not repeat stories covered in recent editions. Check the last few days'

drafts before selecting.

Humanizer check

Before the draft goes to review, it is scanned for AI-writing tells

(staged openers, stock AI words, inflated significance, chatbot residue,

"not X but Y" contrasts, bold-heavy prose) using the pattern list from

blader/humanizer. Flagged lines are

rewritten in plain direct sentences. The check is a pre-pass. A human editor

makes the final call.

After the draft

  • Glossary loop. New companies and concepts from the edition are added to

the site glossary with verified descriptions and source links.

  • Concepts log. The Learn section topic is picked from a starter queue and

logged with the date, so topics rotate without repeating.

  • Search index. The site search index is rebuilt so the new edition is

findable.
