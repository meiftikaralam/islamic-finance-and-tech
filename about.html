About this blog

Most resources on Islamic finance fall into two camps: dense legal texts written for scholars, or high-level summaries written for marketing. Neither tells you how Islamic financial products actually work under the hood—or how to build them in production.

Islamic Finance and Technology exists to bridge that gap.

This site is written for founders, software engineers, product builders, and finance professionals who want to understand how riba-free financial products are structured, regulated, and engineered in the real world. Whether you are launching an Islamic fintech startup, designing payment and ledger workflows inside an institution, or simply learning how halal finance works, the goal is to make complex structures clear and practical.

Every lesson follows a simple rule: plain English, verified sources, and zero filler.

Daily Lessons: Short, practical breakdowns of Islamic banking, sukuk, halal investing, and the software architectures and AI systems used by modern financial institutions.

AAOIFI Standards Library: A growing reference library covering all 62 AAOIFI Shariah Standards in simple, accessible English—free for learning, teaching, and research.

Glossary & Guides: Clear definitions of key contracts, market structures, and screening rules without unnecessary jargon.

There are no ads, paywalls, or paid products here — everything is shared freely fi sabilillah (for the sake of Allah alone) for anyone who wants to learn and build.

About the author

Iftikar is passionate about researching Islamic finance and the engineering behind Islamic finance products. Over the years, he has advised financial institutions on simplifying Islamic product workflows and worked closely with Shariah scholars to develop and launch Islamic financing solutions internationally.

In his engineering career, he has spent over 15 years building enterprise distributed systems, including 7+ years developing critical payment infrastructure at Amazon. There, he helped launch a co-branded credit card program for 1.6 million customers, built payment processing for cashier-less "just walk out" stores, worked on payment fraud detection, and architected data pipelines processing more than 140 TB of data.
