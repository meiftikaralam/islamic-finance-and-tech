Cur8 USD Income Fund Explained: Halal Income in Dollars

By Iftikar · November 24, 2026 · 7 min read

IN THIS POST

Cur8 Capital's USD Income Fund pays a target 9.5% a year from halal, asset-backed deals, open from $5,000. How it works, step by step.

Islamic finance in the USA is growing at a pace the country has not seen before. New funds, new apps, and new platforms are launching every year, and money is coming in from American Muslims and from investors in the Gulf and beyond. One estimate puts the US Islamic finance market at about $250 billion today, across more than 40 institutions, on its way to about $400 billion by 2030.

The public side of the market is well built. The private side stayed thin for a long time. That is the gap Cur8 Capital's USD Income Fund steps into, and it is a real milestone for one simple reason: steady halal income from private deals used to be open only to institutions and very rich investors. This fund opens it in US dollars, from $5,000.

Islamic finance companies in the USA today

The oldest name in US Islamic investing is the Amana Funds, run by Saturna Capital in Washington state. Saturna has applied Islamic screening to its mutual funds since 1986. Amana is a public market fund family, so it buys shares in listed companies.

Wahed runs halal stock portfolios in an app, and in April 2026 it launched a US real estate fund open to ordinary investors from $100. The fund buys rental homes with no debt at all and targets 5 to 7% a year in net returns.

Azzad Asset Management and SP Funds run halal mutual funds and ETFs, and Manzil launched its own halal ETF, MNZL, in November 2025. Guidance Residential, UIF, and Devon Bank lead on home finance, and US Islamic home finance providers have written more than $10 billion of home financing contracts over the years.

The smaller private players are recent. Dhow, founded in 2025 by three brothers in the USA, is a private markets app for Muslim investors, and its first product is a seed stage venture fund called Horizon Fund I. Deenvest offers private real estate deals to accredited investors. Manzil Invest USA runs halal portfolios in an app; Manzil entered the USA in 2024 by buying Aghaz, a Seattle-based halal robo-advisor. On the venture side, Friday Ventures, Alif Capital, and Munch VC back Muslim founders.

So far, the USA has strong halal stock funds and strong home finance. However, it lacked a halal fund that pays regular income from private deals, in dollars, from a small starting amount. Cur8 bridges the gap by building the USD Income Fund.

What is Cur8 USD Income Fund?

Cur8 Capital is the investment arm of Islamic Finance Guru, a UK education platform. Cur8 is authorised and regulated in the UK by the Financial Conduct Authority, and every product on its platform is screened for Shariah compliance by Amanah Advisors and Mufti Adam Faraz. Each deal carries its own Shariah certificate, which investors can read before they put money in.

The USD Income Fund pools investors' money and places it into asset-backed financing deals, all in US dollars. Cur8 set the fund up in 2025. The headline terms are simple:

  • Target return of 9.5% a year, paid twice a year
  • Minimum investment of $5,000
  • No entry fee on the income funds
  • Exit on 3 to 6 months' notice, on a best efforts basis

Please note: the return above is a target return, and it is not guaranteed.

For example, an investor who puts in $10,000 is aiming at $950 a year at the 9.5% target rate, paid as about $475 every six months.

On tax, the USD Income Fund is classed as a non-PFIC fund, based on US tax advice Cur8 took. A PFIC is a foreign fund that earns most of its money from passive sources, and US tax rules make those funds heavy and costly to report. A non-PFIC class means this fund's US investors report their income in the normal way, so the tax reporting stays simpler than most foreign funds.

Customer/Investor journey

Sign up. The investor creates an account on the Cur8 website or in the Cur8 app.

Prove identity. Cur8 runs an identity check through Yoti. The investor scans a passport or driving licence and shows a recent proof of address. This is a legal requirement on every regulated platform.

Read the fund. The investor reads the USD Income Fund page, the fund documents, and the Shariah certificate and Zakat analysis that Cur8 publishes for its deals.

Invest. The investor puts in $5,000 or more. US investors confirm their accredited status at this stage.

Follow the money. Reports and updates land in the app, so the investor can see what the fund holds and what it paid.

Receive income. The fund pays income twice a year.

Exit. The investor gives 3 to 6 months' notice. Cur8 says it has met every redemption request so far, and the exit runs on a best efforts basis, so the timing is not guaranteed.

Where the investor money goes?

The pooled money goes into three kinds of deals. The first is financing for small and growing businesses, such as working capital and trade finance, where a business needs money to buy stock or complete an order. The second is real estate with collateral behind it, where property secures the financing. The third is leasing, where the fund owns an asset, such as a vehicle or equipment, and a business pays to use it.

Cur8's fund page gives three examples of its deals. Erad received financing that helped it unlock follow-on equity funding and expand faster. Faes & Co had its facility structured from scratch to be fully Sharia-compliant. Alif Moliya received support to build its buy now, pay later strategy in a high-growth region, widening access to ethical consumer finance.

Every deal is asset-backed. A real asset stands behind the money, so if a business cannot pay, the fund has a claim on the asset. That is the fund's main protection, and it is also what keeps the structure Shariah-compliant: the return comes from real trade, real property, and real assets, and no interest is charged.

The return reaches investors in a straight line. Businesses and tenants pay the fund under their financing and leasing contracts. The fund collects those payments across all its deals, takes its management fee, and pays the income out to investors twice a year.

Due diligence runs in layers. Cur8's team reviews each business in depth before any money moves: financial forecasts, past accounts, customer agreements, the market the business works in, and its competitors. Industry experts are brought in for a second view when the team wants one. Most applicants are rejected early in the process. A separate Shariah screen then checks the deal itself, and Cur8 turns down deals that raise compliance problems. After the money is in, a Shariah board reviews live deals every quarter, and a full Shariah audit runs once a year.

Comparison with the other investment options

An ordinary investor in the USA now has three doors. The first is a halal stock or sukuk fund, such as the Amana funds or a Shariah ETF: easy to buy, easy to sell, and the value moves with the stock market every day. The second is Wahed's real estate fund: rental homes, no debt, from $100, targeting 5 to 7% a year. The third door is a private income fund like Cur8's: a higher target of 9.5%, money locked in for longer, and an exit that needs months of notice. The higher target pays for the lower liquidity and the higher risk.
