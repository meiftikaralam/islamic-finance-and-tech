# SS 10: Salam and Parallel Salam

## What this standard is about

Salam is a purchase where the buyer pays the full price in advance (the salam capital) for goods delivered at a future date. Parallel salam is a second, separate salam contract in which the bank sells the same goods onward. The two contracts must stay independent of each other.

## Why it exists

Farmers and manufacturers need cash before production. Buyers want future supply. Salam gives advance funding without a loan. Because the goods do not exist yet, the standard demands precise specifications. This removes the uncertainty that could cause disputes.

## The key rules, simply put

- The full price must be paid at the time of contracting.
- The goods must be clearly specified: kind, quality, quantity, and delivery date.
- The goods must be fungible — measurable, weighable, or countable, with no major differences between units — or standardized manufactured goods.
- Salam cannot be for a unique item, land, trees, gold, silver, or currency (when the price was paid in currency), or things valued subjectively like precious stones.
- Delivery may happen in installments. Early delivery is allowed.
- The buyer cannot sell the goods before taking delivery of them.
- In parallel salam, the second contract must be independent. It cannot be conditional on the first.

## An everyday example

A bank pays a wheat farmer $50,000 now for 100 tons of specified-grade wheat to be delivered in six months. The bank then signs a separate parallel salam to sell 100 tons to a miller for $55,000 on the same delivery date. The $5,000 difference is the bank's profit.

## Words to know

- **Salam** — an advance-payment purchase for future delivery.
- **Salam capital** — the price paid in advance.
- **Fungible** — interchangeable units of the same kind, like tons of wheat.
- **Riba** — interest; any guaranteed extra on a loan. Not allowed in Islamic finance.
